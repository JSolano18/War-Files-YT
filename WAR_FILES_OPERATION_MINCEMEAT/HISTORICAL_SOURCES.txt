WAR FILES — THE DEAD MAN WHO FOOLED HITLER (Operation Mincemeat, 1943)
HISTORICAL RESEARCH & SOURCE LIST

HOW THE RESEARCH WAS DONE
The narration was checked against the full texts of the English Wikipedia articles
"Operation Mincemeat" and "Glyndwr Michael" (downloaded 24 Nov 2026; saved in project/research/).
Both articles are extensively referenced to the published histories listed below. The
published books themselves, and the files at The National Archives, were NOT consulted directly
in this production. Before publication, a human should spot-check the key claims below against
Macintyre (2010) and Smyth (2010).

PRINCIPAL PUBLISHED SOURCES (as cited by the reference articles)

- Denis Smyth, Deathly Deception: The Real Story of Operation Mincemeat (Oxford UP, 2010)

CLAIMS TABLE — how each statement is presented
DOCUMENTED FACT
- Trout memo, 29 Sept 1939 (Godfrey; Macintyre: "hallmarks" of Ian Fleming); item 28 title
  and text quoted verbatim.
- Air crash off Cádiz, Sept 1942 (courier James Hadden Turner; body near Tarifa).
- Cholmondeley's "Trojan Horse" (Oct 1942); Twenty Committee rejected it and paired him with Montagu.
- Spilsbury's advice; coroner Bentley Purchase; body found 28 Jan 1943; plan filed 4 Feb 1943.
- Glyndwr Michael: Aberbargoed; father (coal miner) died by suicide when he was 15; mother died
  when he was 31; found ill in a warehouse near King's Cross after eating phosphorus rat poison;
  died at St Pancras Hospital. Age: sources differ (34 vs 36), so the narration says "mid-thirties".
- Legend of Capt. (A/Maj.) William Martin RM, Combined Operations HQ; pocket litter (Pam photo =
  MI5 clerk Jean Leslie; ring receipt £53 10s 6d; Lloyds overdraft £79 19s 2d; theatre stubs; Naval
  and Military Club bill; 18–24 April itinerary); ID photo of Ronnie Reed; cards rubbed for wear;
  uniform worn in by Cholmondeley.
- Nye letter to Alexander (key passage quoted verbatim); Mountbatten letter to Cunningham;
  sardines joke; eyelash; briefcase on a leather-covered chain looped to the trench-coat belt.
- Huelva chosen on hydrographic advice; Abwehr agent Adolf Clauss; vice-consul Francis Haselden.
- Chiefs of Staff approval 13 April; Bevan briefed Churchill 15 April ("give it another swim" —
  presented as Bevan's recollection); Eisenhower's HQ confirmation 17 April.
- 17 April: body dressed; canister with dry ice labelled "Handle with care: optical instruments";
  driven overnight by St John "Jock" Horsfall to Greenock; HMS Seraph (Lt. N. L. A. "Bill" Jewell)
  sailed 19 April, bombed twice, off Huelva 29 April, body released 04:15 on 30 April; Psalm 39;
  signal "Mincemeat completed".
- Found ~09:30 by a fisherman; naval judge; post-mortem 1 May ("asphyxiation through immersion
  in the sea"); buried 2 May with military honours.
- Briefcase: San Fernando, Madrid; Kühlenthal and Canaris; letters extracted with a probe; passed
  to Germans 8 May; returned 11 May; eyelash missing; folding/curl evidence; reassuring cable.
- Ultra decrypt (message of 12 May, decrypted 14 May); Hollis signal "Mincemeat swallowed rod,
  line and sinker by the right people" (quoted in part).
- Dönitz's record of 14 May meeting with Hitler ("Anglo-Saxon order"; Sardinia & Peloponnese).
- Hitler's orders: Greece, Sardinia, Corsica defended; 1st Panzer Division France → Greece; Sardinia
  doubled to ~10,000; torpedo boats moved; 21 aircraft left Sicily for Sardinia 4 hours into Husky;
  Rommel sent to Greece late July.
- Husky 9 July 1943; ~65,000 Germans vs ~400,000 Allied troops; Sicily fell in 38 days; Mussolini
  ousted 25 July.
- Montagu OBE 1944; Cholmondeley MBE 1948; Duff Cooper's novel 1950; Montagu's book 1953 (two
  million copies), film 1956; Montagu's "ne'er-do-well" quote; headstone wording; 1996 identification;
  CWGC added "Glyndwr Michael served as Major William Martin, RM" (1997/98 — narration says "late
  1990s"); Aberbargoed memorial plaque "Y Dyn Na Fu Erioed".
ATTRIBUTED / INTERPRETIVE (always credited in narration)
- Casualty and shipping expectations vs. outcome, and the 90 vs 38 days — attributed to Ben Macintyre.
- "Perhaps the most successful single deception operation of the entire war" — Michael Howard,
  together with his caveat about Hitler's Balkan obsession; Latimer's point about Hitler's distrust
  of the Italians; Macintyre: exact impact "impossible to calculate".
- Churchill's "everyone but a bloody fool would know it's Sicily" — presented as "reputed".
- "Then the largest amphibious assault of the war" — a widely made assessment of Husky.
DISPUTED (presented as disputed)
- Montagu's claim that the family gave permission — historians (Macintyre; Pukas) found no
  evidence; Macintyre states no relatives were found.
- Alternative identities (HMS Dasher sailor: John Melville / Tom Martin) — mentioned, with the
  note that official records and most historians name Glyndwr Michael.
DELIBERATELY NOT CLAIMED
- That Mincemeat "won the war" or single-handedly decided Sicily.
- Any invented dialogue. The only quotations used are documented ones listed above.
- Exact wording of the pre-scripted Haselden–Hillgarth cables (shown only as redacted recreations).

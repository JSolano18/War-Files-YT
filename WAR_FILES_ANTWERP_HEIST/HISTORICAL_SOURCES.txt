WAR FILES — THE ANTWERP DIAMOND HEIST — HISTORICAL SOURCES & FACT-CHECK LOG

RESEARCH LIMITS (stated plainly)
Production ran in a sandboxed cloud environment. Direct page fetches were blocked for wired.com,
wikipedia.org, guinnessworldrecords.com, nationalgeographic.com, expatica.com and jckonline.com.
Facts were therefore checked through web-search result summaries that cross-referenced these
sources. No court records were consulted directly. Before publishing, a human should re-read the
WIRED feature and the Wikipedia article and check them against the claims table below.

CLAIMS TABLE: how each statement is presented in the narration
CONFIRMED (multiple sources agree)
- Location: Antwerp Diamond Centre, Schupstraat 9–11, Antwerp, Belgium (not Germany; not a retail bank).
- Weekend of 15–16 February 2003; discovered on the morning of Monday 17 February 2003.
- The vault was below street level (widely reported as two floors down).
- Notarbartolo rented an office in the building about two years beforehand.
- Rubbish from the crew was dumped in woodland beside the E19 near Vilvoorde and found by the
  land's caretaker, August Van Camp, who called police after seeing Diamond Centre envelopes.
- A half-eaten salami sandwich in the rubbish yielded DNA.
- Notarbartolo was arrested on 21 February 2003 when he returned to the Diamond Centre.
- 2005: Notarbartolo was sentenced to 10 years; D'Onorio, Finotto and Tavano to 5 years each.
- Notarbartolo was paroled in 2009.
- Guinness lists it as the largest diamond heist ($100 million). Most of the loot was never recovered.
- The "King of Keys" was never identified.
WIDELY REPORTED (stated with "reportedly" / "accounts describe" / "by widely cited estimates")
- "Ten layers of security": combination dial with 100 million codes, key ~1 ft long, infrared heat
  detectors, seismic sensor, Doppler radar, light sensor, magnetic field, box locks, guards/cameras.
- Antwerp handling roughly 80% of the world's rough diamonds in that era.
- Nicknames and roles of the crew (the Genius, the Monster, Speedy, the King of Keys).
DISPUTED (presented as a range or as disputed)
- Boxes opened: 109 vs 123; total boxes reported between 160 and 189.
- Whose DNA was on the sandwich: sources differ (Notarbartolo vs Finotto). The narration says
  "accounts differ".
- The value: ~$100M (widely reported) vs Notarbartolo's ~€18M/$20M.
NOTARBARTOLO'S ACCOUNT (always attributed to him; not confirmed by Belgian authorities)
- Months of reconnaissance; photos with a camera hidden in a pen; a hidden camera above the vault
  door to record the combination; a replica for rehearsal; entry from a neighboring property at the
  back; hairspray on the heat sensor; tape on the light sensor; an aluminum plate holding the
  magnetic sensor closed; the real key kept near the vault; "Speedy" panicking and scattering the
  trash; hired by a diamond merchant; insurance fraud.
DELIBERATELY OMITTED (could not be verified)
- Any 2013 re-arrest or later detentions; specific tools used on the boxes; a videotape among the
  rubbish (single low-reliability source); the court venue and exact verdict date.

NO INVENTED MATERIAL
No dialogue, eyewitness quotes, photographs, headlines or investigative findings were invented.
Every reconstructed scene is labelled on screen.

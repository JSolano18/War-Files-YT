[01:01]  ══ CHAPTER I — THE DIAMOND CAPITAL ══

   (visual: map)
[01:05]  To understand this crime, you first have to understand the city.
[01:09]  Antwerp sits in the north of Belgium, on the river Scheldt, about forty-five kilometers from Brussels.
[01:17]  For more than five hundred years, its fortunes have been tied to one thing above all else.
[01:22]  Diamonds.

   (visual: city_night — ILLUSTRATION)
[01:25]  By the early two thousands, Antwerp was the center of the global diamond trade.
[01:30]  By widely cited estimates, roughly eighty percent of the world's rough diamonds passed through this one city.

   (visual: district — SIMPLIFIED ILLUSTRATION — NOT TO SCALE)
[01:38]  Most of that trade happens inside a district of just a few streets beside Antwerp's Central Station.
[01:45]  Hoveniersstraat. Schupstraat. Rijfstraat.
[01:48]  Traders of dozens of nationalities work side by side in these few blocks — rough stones arriving from mines around the world, polished stones leaving for jewelers everywhere.
[02:00]  Here, fortunes in stones change hands every day — and deals are traditionally sealed with a handshake and a single word: mazal.

   (visual: statement)
[02:10]  The district is watched by cameras, patrolled by police, and protected by barriers against vehicle traffic.

   (visual: building — SIMPLIFIED ILLUSTRATION)
[02:18]  And at its heart, at Schupstraat 9 to 11, stood the Antwerp Diamond Centre.
[02:24]  Its tenants were diamond dealers. And two floors below street level, behind a massive steel door, was the vault where many of them kept their most valuable goods.
[02:35]  To the people who trusted it, that vault was about as safe as a place could be.


[02:41]  ══ CHAPTER II — THE TENANT ══

   (visual: map)
[02:44]  Around two years before the robbery, a new tenant moved into the building.
[02:49]  He was Italian. He presented himself as a diamond dealer. And he rented an office inside the Antwerp Diamond Centre.

   (visual: dossier — ILLUSTRATION)
[02:58]  His name was Leonardo Notarbartolo.
[03:02]  As a tenant, he had what every legitimate dealer in the building had: an office, a safe-deposit box of his own, and a reason to walk into the vault area.
[03:13]  To the guards and the other dealers, he was a familiar face. Polite. Unremarkable. The kind of man nobody looks at twice.
[03:21]  A Belgian court would later conclude that he was something else entirely: the man who organized the robbery.

   (visual: statement)
[03:29]  Notarbartolo did not work alone.
[03:32]  He was connected to a group of thieves based around Turin, in northern Italy — a crew the press would later call the School of Turin.

   (visual: crew — ILLUSTRATION)
[03:42]  The men convicted alongside him became known by nicknames that sound as if they were written for a movie.
[03:49]  Elio D'Onorio, called the Genius — described as an expert in alarms and electronics.
[03:55]  Ferdinando Finotto, the Monster — described as a lock specialist, a skilled driver, and a man of enormous strength.
[04:03]  Pietro Tavano, known as Speedy — described as Notarbartolo's longtime friend.
[04:09]  And a fifth man, an older specialist known only as the King of Keys, said to be one of the finest key forgers alive. His identity has never been established.

   (visual: statement)
[04:22]  One warning before we go further.
[04:24]  Much of what is known about these men — including their nicknames and roles — comes from Notarbartolo's own later account of the heist.
[04:34]  And as we'll see, his version of events is not the only one.


[04:38]  ══ CHAPTER III — THE PREPARATION ══

   (visual: layers)
[04:42]  Breaking into the vault meant defeating a system that, on paper, looked overwhelming.
[04:48]  Accounts of the heist describe it in layers.
[04:51]  A combination dial with one hundred million possible codes.
[04:56]  A massive steel door, opened with a key reportedly about a foot long.
[05:01]  Infrared heat detectors. A seismic sensor to pick up vibration. Doppler radar. A light sensor. And a magnetic field that would trigger the alarm if the door was opened.
[05:13]  Inside, every safe-deposit box had a lock of its own.
[05:17]  And beyond all of that: guards, cameras, and the police presence of the diamond district itself.

   (visual: statement)
[05:25]  For a thief, the problem is not only how to beat each layer. It is how to learn what the layers are — without anyone noticing that you are looking.

   (visual: corridor — DRAMATIZATION)
[05:35]  According to Notarbartolo's later account, the answer was patience.
[05:40]  As a tenant, he could go down to the vault again and again, like any other dealer — and he said he spent many months studying it.
[05:48]  He claimed he secretly photographed the vault, using a tiny camera concealed inside a pen.

   (visual: blueprint — ILLUSTRATION — BASED ON NOTARBARTOLO'S ACCOUNT)
[05:55]  In his telling, one step mattered more than any other.
[05:59]  He said the crew hid a small camera of their own above the vault door — positioned to record the guards as they dialed the combination.
[06:08]  If that account is true, the vault's first line of defense was defeated without anyone touching it.

   (visual: blueprint — ILLUSTRATION — BASED ON NOTARBARTOLO'S ACCOUNT)
[06:15]  He also described a rehearsal: the crew practicing on a replica of the vault's defenses, until every step could be performed quickly and in near silence.
[06:26]  Belgian investigators have never confirmed every detail of this version.
[06:32]  What is not in doubt is that whoever entered that vault knew exactly what they would face.

   (visual: highway — DRAMATIZATION)
[06:38]  By February 2003, the plan was set.
[06:42]  The target was a weekend — two days when the offices would be closed and the building would be at its quietest.


[06:50]  ══ CHAPTER IV — INSIDE THE VAULT ══

   (visual: building — ILLUSTRATION — BASED ON NOTARBARTOLO'S ACCOUNT)
[06:53]  According to the account Notarbartolo later gave to WIRED magazine, the crew did not walk in through the front entrance on the street.
[07:02]  They approached from a neighboring property at the back — away from the cameras that watched the street.

   (visual: sensors — ILLUSTRATION — BASED ON NOTARBARTOLO'S ACCOUNT)
[07:09]  Every defense, he claimed, had a weakness.
[07:12]  The heat sensor, the story goes, was neutralized with something as ordinary as hairspray — a thin coating that briefly insulated the detector.
[07:22]  The light sensor was covered with tape.
[07:25]  And the magnetic sensor on the vault door — the alarm that should have sounded the moment the door moved — was reportedly bypassed by holding its two halves together with an aluminum plate, so the circuit never broke.

   (visual: key — ILLUSTRATION)
[07:39]  And the famous key?
[07:41]  In Notarbartolo's account, the crew never needed to forge it at all.
[07:45]  He said the real key was kept close to the vault itself — where anyone who knew to look could find it.

   (visual: statement)
[07:53]  If that is true, one of the most sophisticated vaults in the world was undone by a simple human habit.
[08:00]  It is a claim that has never been independently proven. But what happened next is beyond dispute.


[08:08]  ══ CHAPTER V — THE WEEKEND ══

   (visual: timeline)
[08:12]  The break-in took place over the weekend of February 15th and 16th, 2003.
[08:18]  By Monday morning, it would be discovered. By the following Friday, there would be an arrest.

   (visual: city_night — DRAMATIZATION)
[08:24]  That weekend, the diamond district fell quiet.
[08:28]  The trading halls were empty. The offices were dark.

   (visual: corridor — DRAMATIZATION)
[08:32]  Two levels below the street, the thieves reached the vault door.
[08:36]  With the combination known, the key in hand, and the sensors neutralized, the door that was supposed to stop everyone simply swung open.

   (visual: vault_door — DRAMATIZATION)
[08:47]  Inside were rows of safe-deposit boxes — and the crew began forcing them open, one after another, for hours.

   (visual: boxes_wall — ILLUSTRATION)

   (visual: bars)

   (visual: diamonds — ILLUSTRATION)

[09:21]  Much of what was in those boxes was never documented.
[09:25]  In the diamond trade, discretion is part of the business — which makes the true size of the loss almost impossible to measure.

   (visual: highway — DRAMATIZATION)
[09:34]  Before Monday morning, the crew was gone, leaving behind a vault in chaos.
[09:39]  And somewhere between Antwerp and Brussels, they made a decision that would cost them everything.


[09:46]  ══ CHAPTER VI — MONDAY MORNING ══

   (visual: boxes_wall — ILLUSTRATION)
[09:50]  On the morning of Monday, February 17th, the robbery was discovered.
[09:55]  The vault was in chaos: box doors hanging open, paper and envelopes scattered across the floor.
[10:02]  Antwerp police — including the specialized unit dedicated to the diamond trade, known as the Diamond Squad — were called in.

   (visual: statement)
[10:11]  At first, investigators faced a crime with almost no starting point. The vault door had not been blasted or cut. There was no obvious trail to follow.

   (visual: forest — ILLUSTRATION)
[10:23]  But that same morning, some thirty-five kilometers to the south, something unusual turned up in a patch of woodland beside the E19 highway, near Vilvoorde, just outside Brussels.

   (visual: map)
[10:36]  The land was looked after by a local man named August Van Camp.
[10:41]  And on his property, he found a pile of rubbish that someone had dumped among the trees.

   (visual: forest — ILLUSTRATION)
[10:47]  Among the garbage were envelopes bearing the name of the Antwerp Diamond Centre.
[10:52]  Van Camp called the police.


[10:55]  ══ CHAPTER VII — THE EVIDENCE ══

   (visual: evidence — ILLUSTRATION)
[10:59]  What investigators recovered from those woods would become the core of the case.
[11:04]  There were torn envelopes connected to the diamond centre.
[11:08]  There were documents and other items that could be traced to the people who had thrown them away.
[11:14]  And there was a half-eaten salami sandwich.

   (visual: evidence — ILLUSTRATION)
[11:18]  The sandwich carried DNA. Investigators tied it to a member of the crew — although accounts differ on exactly whose DNA it was.
[11:27]  Other items pointed directly to Leonardo Notarbartolo.

   (visual: statement)
[11:33]  It was a staggering contrast. A crew that had allegedly outwitted one of the most secure vaults on the planet had been exposed by a pile of trash in the woods.

   (visual: forest — DRAMATIZATION)
[11:45]  Notarbartolo later described the dumping as a panicked mistake.
[11:50]  In his account, Speedy — nervous and exhausted — scattered the garbage in the woods instead of getting rid of it as planned.
[11:58]  Whatever the reason, it handed investigators a direct line to the suspects.


[12:04]  ══ CHAPTER VIII — TWO VERSIONS ══

   (visual: timeline)
[12:08]  On February 21st, 2003 — just five days after the break-in — Notarbartolo returned to the Antwerp Diamond Centre.
[12:17]  Detectives were waiting for him. He was arrested.
[12:21]  From there, investigators turned to the men around him.

   (visual: crew — ILLUSTRATION)
[12:26]  In 2005, a Belgian court convicted Notarbartolo of organizing the heist and sentenced him to ten years in prison.
[12:35]  Elio D'Onorio, Ferdinando Finotto, and Pietro Tavano each received five years.
[12:41]  The King of Keys was never identified.

   (visual: split)
[12:46]  Then, in 2009, Notarbartolo told his side of the story to WIRED magazine. And it reframed everything.
[12:54]  He claimed the crew had been hired by a diamond merchant.
[12:58]  He claimed the robbery was part of an insurance fraud scheme.
[13:02]  And he claimed the haul was far smaller than reported — around eighteen million euros, or roughly twenty million dollars.

   (visual: bars)
[13:11]  Many have doubted his story. Belgian authorities have never confirmed it, and the merchant he described has never been identified to the satisfaction of the courts.
[13:22]  He also had every reason to minimize what was stolen.
[13:25]  And yet his version is hard to dismiss completely — because no one has ever been able to prove exactly what was inside those boxes.


[13:35]  ══ CHAPTER IX — THE MISSING DIAMONDS ══

   (visual: diamonds — ILLUSTRATION)
[13:39]  Notarbartolo was released on parole in 2009.
[13:43]  And the diamonds? The overwhelming majority have never been recovered.
[13:49]  Diamonds are small, valuable, and difficult to trace. Once they are recut or quietly sold, a stone can vanish into the legitimate market almost without a trace.

[14:05]  A brilliant theft? Or a fraud dressed up as one?
[14:09]  Who was the King of Keys?

   (visual: vault_door — ILLUSTRATION)

[14:21]  But neither has answered the question that matters most.

[14:34]  It has inspired books, documentaries, and endless speculation.
[14:39]  But the most important part of the story is still locked away — not in a vault, but in the silence of the people who know where the diamonds went.
